01 / HIS JOURNEY

A Life of Purpose.

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam (15 October 1931 – 27 July 2015) was an Indian aerospace scientist and statesman who served as the 11th President of India from 2002 to 2007. Born in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, Kalam studied physics and aerospace engineering. He spent decades contributing to India's scientific development through the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) and the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). He played an important role in India's civilian space programme and military missile development. His contributions to science and technology earned him the title "Missile Man of India". Beyond his scientific achievements, he was a teacher, author and inspirational figure who encouraged generations of young Indians to dream big and work towards a better future.

02 / THE MILESTONES

A Journey Through Time.

Every great journey begins with a dream. Here are the moments that shaped his legacy.

1931

A Humble Beginning

Born on 15 October in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, into a modest family.

1954

Academic Foundation

Completed his physics degree at St. Joseph's College, Tiruchirappalli.

1960

The Beginning of Flight

Graduated in aeronautical engineering from the Madras Institute of Technology.

1980

A Historic Achievement

Under his leadership, India's SLV-III successfully placed the Rohini satellite into orbit.

1983

Missile Development

Led the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme, advancing India's defence capabilities.

1997

Bharat Ratna

Honoured with India's highest civilian award for his outstanding contributions.

2002

The People's President

Became the 11th President of India, serving the nation until 2007.

2015

An Eternal Legacy

Passed away on 27 July while delivering a lecture at IIM Shillong. His legacy continues to inspire millions.
